01 · How it works

Three beats, and you're done.

No window to open, no text box to click into. Vocs lives in the menu bar and gets out of the way.

  1. 1

    Press ⌥Space

    Anywhere, in any app. Toggle it, or hold the key to talk. A small pill shows it's listening.

  2. 2

    Say what you mean

    Vocs transcribes while you speak, not after, so a five-minute monologue finishes as fast as a sentence.

  3. 3

    Press it again

    The text is pasted into whatever had focus, and your clipboard is put back as it was.

02 · What you get

Small app. Careful details.

Quiet in meetings

On a call, the other people come out of your speakers and into your microphone. While you dictate, Vocs mutes the built-in speakers, and puts them back the moment you stop. Headphones are left alone.

99 languages

Let Vocs detect the language, or pin one. Switch mid-day without touching a setting.

Your dictionary

Teach it the names and jargon it gets wrong. It biases recognition and corrects the output.

Fixes, not rewrites

Fillers go, real words stay. Optional on-device grammar fixes only the slips that are wrong every time.

A notebook of everything

Every dictation is kept locally, searchable, and editable like a note. A failed paste is never a lost thought.

Escape takes it back

Press Escape and you get a three-second countdown to cancel. Press it again and the recording resumes with nothing lost.

Crash-safe

The transcript is saved as it grows, so even a crash mid-sentence keeps what you had said.

04 · Privacy

Nothing leaves your Mac.

Recognition runs on your machine. There is no account to make, no subscription, no word limit, and no telemetry. The code is open, so you can check.

  • Your audio stays hereIt is never uploaded anywhere. Recognition happens on this machine.
  • Two kinds of requestDownloading a model, and an update check you can switch off. Nothing else.
  • Signed updatesVocs refuses any update that doesn't verify against the key built into the app.

05 · Install

Up and running in a minute.

Needs macOS 13 or later, Apple Silicon or Intel, and about 600 MB of disk for the speech model.

Apple SiliconM1, M2, M3, M4 and newer Download IntelIntel-based Macs Download

One universal download: the same file runs natively on both. Not sure which you have? Apple menu → About This Mac.

· Release notes

  1. Open the downloaded DMG. macOS will say it can't verify Vocs-macOS.dmg. That's expected: Vocs is free and independent, so it isn't notarised by Apple. Click Done, not Move to Trash.
  2. Approve it in System Settings. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down to the message about the DMG and choose Open Anyway, then enter your password. The DMG opens.
  3. Drag Vocs to Applications and open it. macOS may ask once more, this time about the app. Same place, same Open Anyway. After that it never asks again, and updates inside the app don't ask either.
  4. Follow the setup. Allow the microphone, download the model, and try your hotkey.

Prefer the terminal? Run this before opening the DMG, and again for the app if macOS asks about it:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine ~/Downloads/Vocs-macOS.dmg
xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Vocs.app

    07 · Questions

    Good to know.

    Is it really free?

    Yes. No account, no subscription, no word cap. It is open source under the MIT licence.

    Does it work offline?

    Yes. After the one-time model download, dictation needs no connection at all.

    Why does macOS warn me when I open it?

    Apple notarisation requires a paid developer account, and Vocs is a free, independent project. The app is signed, and the steps above approve the DMG and the app once each. After that macOS remembers.

    How do updates work?

    Vocs checks quietly at launch and once a day (and tries again soon if the network was not ready), and Settings → Updates has a Check for updates button. When a new version is ready it waits in a bar at the bottom of the window: press Install and restart and Vocs relaunches by itself. Every update is verified against a signature before it is installed, and it won't install in the middle of a dictation. You can turn the check off.

    Can I take words back while I'm talking?

    Yes, in English. Say “scratch that” to drop the sentence you just spoke, “undo last sentence” for the one before it, or “scratch everything” to start the dictation again. The words are removed before anything is pasted. It is part of Spoken formatting in Settings.

    What does it need permission for?

    The microphone, to hear you. Accessibility is optional: it lets Vocs paste for you. Without it, your text goes to the clipboard instead.

    Can I help build it?

    Please do. Read the contributing guide and open an issue or a pull request.
